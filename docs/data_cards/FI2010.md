# FI-2010 data card

## Intended use

FI-2010 is the primary supervised benchmark for three-class short-horizon mid-price movement classification. It is used here as a research benchmark only, not as evidence of a tradable strategy.

## Provenance and coverage

The dataset was introduced by Ntakaris et al. in *Benchmark Dataset for Mid-Price Forecasting of Limit Order Book Data with Machine Learning Methods* (arXiv:1705.03233). It covers five Nasdaq Nordic instruments over ten trading days in June 2010. The public representation contains approximately 394,000 rows; each row represents ten underlying market events.

The repository does not redistribute the data. Source-file and materialized-file SHA-256 hashes are generated by `scripts/materialize_fi2010.py` and retained in the local materialization manifest.

## Representation and labels

The primary input is `NoAuction_DecPre`: 10 levels times ask price, ask size, bid price, and bid size, for 40 columns. A 100-row sequence is used. Shipped labels are primary. Their documented forward-mean horizons `k={1,2,3,5,10}` correspond to `{10,20,30,50,100}` underlying events. This study reports 10, 20, 50, and 100.

## Splits and leakage controls

Days 1–6 train, day 7 validates, and days 8–10 test. Windows are confined to inferred `(stock, day)` segments. Validation and test use a 200-event purge. Model selection cannot construct test datasets, and all 16 selected configurations are hash-locked before final scripts may load test data.

## Limitations

- Public normalization prevents recovery of confidential raw prices.
- The public rows compress ten underlying events, so they are not a message-by-message book.
- Stock identity is not shipped as a column and is reconstructed from large, audited price seams.
- The ten-day, five-stock sample is narrow in market, era, and regime.
- Published DeepLOB numbers use materially different normalization, validation, and training protocols, so they are contextual anchors rather than exact reproduction targets.
